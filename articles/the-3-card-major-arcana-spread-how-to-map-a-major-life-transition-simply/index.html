Life rarely shifts in neat, predictable steps. Usually, big changes arrive in messy clusters—a sudden job layoff that forces a career pivot, a cross-country move, or the quiet realization that a long-term relationship has run its course. When I find myself standing at one of these crossroads, the standard ten-card Celtic Cross spread can feel overwhelming and overly complicated.

That is why I always default to a simple 3-card Major Arcana spread when mapping a major life transition.

By pulling exclusively from the 21 cards of the Major Arcana—the heavy hitters of the tarot deck that represent life themes, spiritual lessons, and archetypal milestones—you cut straight through the daily noise. This spread does not tell you what time to catch a flight or whether your neighbor likes you. Instead, it diagnoses the deeper current of where you are, what is shifting beneath your feet, and where you are landing.

Why Use Only the Major Arcana?

Most tarot readings mix the Minor Arcana (the day-to-day weather of your life) with the Major Arcana (the major tectonic plates moving underneath). When you are in a massive life transition, you do not need a weather report. You need to know if you are standing on a fault line.

Separating out the 56 Minor Arcana cards and shuffling only the Major Arcana forces the reading to address the existential weight of your situation. If you pull Death, The Tower, or The Fool during a career transition, you instantly know that this is not just a minor job change—it is a complete reinvention of your professional identity.

Setting Up Your Space and Question

Before you touch the cards, you need a sharp, specific focus. A vague question like “What is my future?” will yield a vague, useless answer.

Instead, frame your question around the specific transition you are navigating. For example: “What is the core lesson of this career pivot?” or “What do I need to understand about this impending cross-country move?”

Take the 22 Major Arcana cards out of your deck. Shuffle them thoroughly while keeping your question clearly in mind. Cut the deck into three piles with your left hand, restack them, and deal three cards face down in a horizontal row from left to right.

Card 1: Where You Stand Right Now

Turn over the first card on the left. This represents your current relationship to the transition. It highlights the psychological or emotional anchor holding you in place—or dragging behind you.

Imagine you pulled The Hanged Man for this position. This tells you immediately that your current frustration stems from a forced pause. You might feel stuck, but the card reveals that your immediate reality requires surrender, not frantic action. You are between phases, and trying to force doors open right now will only exhaust you.

Another common scenario: pulling The Chariot for Card 1. This shows you are gripping the reins too tightly, trying to control every moving part of a chaotic relocation or breakup through sheer willpower alone.

Card 2: The Hidden Friction or Catalyst

Flip the middle card. This is the friction point. It represents the obstacle, the uncomfortable truth, or the internal resistance you are trying to ignore. Major life transitions rarely fail because of external logistics; they stall because of our own internal friction.

Let us say you are debating whether to leave a toxic corporate job to start your own business, and you pull The Devil for Card 2. This card is not here to scare you; it is a mirror reflecting your attachment to security, status, or a predictable paycheck. The friction isn’t the market—it is your fear of stepping outside familiar handcuffs.

Or perhaps you pull Wheel of Fortune in the middle position. This indicates that your friction is a resistance to pure, unadulterated randomness. You are fighting a shift that is entirely outside your control, wishing things were different instead of adapting to the new rules of the game.

Card 3: The Emerging Horizon

The final card on the right points toward the integration or the immediate horizon. It does not predict a fixed future, but rather shows the energy you are growing into as you navigate the transition.

Picture pulling The Star in this third position after a grueling personal heartbreak or health scare. The Star brings radical hope, healing, and clarity after a storm. It tells you that your transition is clearing out dead weight to make room for a renewed sense of purpose.

If you pull The Fool, it signals that the other side of your transition is a blank slate. You are about to step off a metaphorical cliff, but your past experiences have equipped you with everything you need to begin again with fresh optimism.

  • Card 1 (Left): Your current stance or psychological anchor.
  • Card 2 (Middle): The hidden obstacle, fear, or catalyst driving the friction.
  • Card 3 (Right): The emerging theme or mindset waiting on the other side.

Reading the Story Across the Board

Once all three cards are face up, step back and look at them as a sequence sentence. Tarot is a narrative art.

If your spread runs The Tower (Card 1) to Death (Card 2) to The Sun (Card 3), you are looking at a profound, radical restructuring of your life. The structure has already broken down (The Tower), the old identity is shedding right now (Death), and radical clarity and vitality are waiting for you once you stop fighting the rubble (The Sun). You do not need to memorize textbook definitions to feel the narrative arc of those three cards working together.
