When a relationship hits a confusing crossroads—whether you are deciding to define the status, move in together, or have a difficult conversation about the future—the urge to text three different friends for advice is strong. But sometimes, outside opinions just muddy the waters. That is why I turn to a simple 3-card tarot spread when I need to untangle my own relationship thoughts.

You do not need a massive layout to get clarity. In fact, a three-card pull is often more effective than a Celtic Cross because it forces you to focus strictly on the core of the issue. Here is how I set up, draw, and read a three-card spread specifically for relationship decisions, using a real-world example from my own practice.

Choose Your Question Wisely

The secret to a useful tarot reading is asking a sharp, grounded question. Vague questions yield vague cards. If you ask, “What is my love life going to look like?”, you will likely get a confusing puzzle of archetypes that do not help you make a concrete choice.

Instead, frame your question around your agency and the current dynamic. For instance, imagine a common scenario: Sarah has been dating Mark for six months. Mark travels constantly for work, and Sarah is trying to decide if she should bring up moving in together or pump the brakes.

Instead of asking, “Does Mark want to marry me?”, Sarah’s question should be: “What do I need to understand about the current trajectory of this relationship before I bring up moving in together?”

Notice how this puts the focus on clarity and timing, rather than trying to mind-read a partner.

Lay Out the Cards

Shuffle your deck using whatever method feels natural to you—riffle, overhand, or spreading the cards face down across a table and mixing them up. As you shuffle, keep your specific question in mind. Once it feels complete, cut the deck into three piles with your left hand (traditionally the receiving hand), stack them back together, and pull three cards from the top, placing them left to right in a straight line.

For relationship decisions, I use this specific chronological and thematic structure:

  • Card 1 (Left): The Current Reality. This represents the underlying truth of where the dynamic stands right now, stripped of wishful thinking or anxiety.
  • Card 2 (Middle): The Obstacle or Blind Spot. This highlights what you might be ignoring, fearing, or failing to communicate effectively.
  • Card 3 (Right): The Potential Outcome/Next Step. This shows where the situation is heading if you continue on your current path, or what energy you need to step into.

Sticking with Sarah’s situation regarding Mark, let us look at how this plays out in practice with actual cards.

Read the Spread Step-by-Step

Let us say Sarah draws the following cards for her question about moving in together:

  1. The Current Reality: The Two of Pentacles
  2. The Obstacle: The Eight of Swords
  3. The Next Step: The Page of Cups

Now, translate these symbols into plain English rather than getting bogged down in memorized textbook definitions.

The Two of Pentacles is all about juggling, balancing priorities, and shifting schedules. For Sarah, this immediately reflects the reality of Mark’s heavy travel schedule and her own busy career. They are constantly trying to coordinate calendars just to see each other on weekends. The card says plainly: Your current routine is characterized by a lot of logistical movement.

Next, look at the obstacle: The Eight of Swords. This card depicts a figure bound and blindfolded, surrounded by a cage of swords, though the bonds are loose enough to remove. Sarah realizes that her hesitation to bring up the move isn’t just about Mark’s schedule—she has been trapping herself in mental worst-case scenarios. She is afraid that if she brings up the topic, she will sound demanding or push him away, so she has kept herself paralyzed in silence.

Finally, the Page of Cups points to the next step. Pages bring messages, and Cups represent emotions, vulnerability, and fresh emotional starts. This tells Sarah that her next move shouldn’t be an ultimatum or a heavy, formal sit-down over a spreadsheet of rent costs. Instead, it is an invitation to have an open-hearted, curious conversation about feelings without any rigid expectations attached.

Synthesize and Take Action

A tarot reading is a mirror, not a crystal ball. It does not make the decision for you; it organizes your thoughts so you can make a clear-headed choice.

Looking at her spread, Sarah realized that the timing (Two of Pentacles) really wasn’t ideal for signing a joint lease right this second, but her fear of communication (Eight of Swords) was keeping her from having an honest talk about the future. Using the energy of the Page of Cups, she decided to bring up the topic casually over dinner, framing it as a fun brainstorm about their future rather than an immediate demand to pack boxes.

When you do your own reading, take a photo of the cards on your phone, write down your immediate gut reactions in a journal, and give yourself twenty-four hours to let the message settle before you take action.
