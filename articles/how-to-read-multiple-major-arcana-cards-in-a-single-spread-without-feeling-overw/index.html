Drawing a single tarot card is straightforward, but pulling a three-card or Celtic cross spread and finding three, four, or even five Major Arcana staring back at you can cause instant panic. When I first started reading tarot, seeing multiple heavy-hitters like The Tower, Death, and The Devil in the same layout made me want to sweep the deck off the table and pretend nothing happened.

The Major Arcana represent massive life themes, karmic lessons, and spiritual turning points. Naturally, getting a handful of them at once feels like the universe is shouting through a megaphone. Over years of practice, I have learned that a heavy Major Arcana spread is not a curse. It is simply a moment of high focus. Here is how I break down these intense spreads without letting the overwhelm take over.

Shift Your Perspective From Doom to Big Picture

The biggest mistake beginners make with the Major Arcana is assuming every card spells literal doom. When The Tower or The Hanged Man appears, our brains jump straight to worst-case scenarios.

Instead, view the Major Arcana as a weather report for your overall life direction rather than a list of inescapable events. If you see multiple major cards, it means your current situation involves a core life lesson or a pivotal transition. Minor Arcana cards deal with the daily grind—work deadlines, minor arguments, cash flow. Major Arcana cards deal with identity, major pivots, and spiritual growth. When you get a pile of them, recognize that you are standing at a major intersection. That is exciting, even if it feels chaotic.

Look for the Narrative Thread

When you have three or four major cards in a spread, do not try to read them in total isolation. They are usually telling a chronological or causal story.

For example, imagine a three-card spread about a career change, and you pull The Chariot (position one, current energy), The Tower (position two, the obstacle), and The Star (position three, the outcome).

  • The Chariot shows you driving hard toward a specific goal, pushing full speed ahead.
  • The Tower reveals that your current momentum is about to hit an unexpected wall, dismantling the very structure you built.
  • The Star shows that once the dust clears, you will find renewed hope, healing, and a much more authentic path forward.

Instead of getting stuck on the terrifying imagery of The Tower, look at how it bridges the ambition of The Chariot with the peace of The Star. The cards are saying: your current relentless push needs a sudden shake-up so you can find what truly fulfills you. Connect the dots like chapters in a book.

Isolate and Anchor with Minor Arcana

If your spread is an overwhelming sea of Major Arcana, look for any Minor Arcana cards present to act as your anchor. Minor cards bring practical, grounded energy to the table.

If you pull four Major Arcana and one single Two of Pentacles, pay close attention to that Two of Pentacles. That humble minor card tells you how to handle the massive spiritual shifts happening around you: by juggling your daily responsibilities, balancing your budget, or staying adaptable on a practical level. If your spread is 100% Major Arcana with zero minors, that signals a period where daily routines take a back seat to deep, internal transformation. Knowing that helps you grant yourself permission to step back from mundane multitasking.

Count Your Archetypes and Elements

Sometimes a quick visual scan helps calm the nerves. Look at the specific flavors of the major cards you pulled.

  • Do you have cards representing movement and action (like The Chariot or The Fool)?
  • Do you have cards representing stillness and introspection (like The Hermit or The High Priestess)?

If most of your major cards are passive or reflective, your spread is telling you to stop forcing things and sit tight. If they are active, it is time to pack your bags and make a move. Grouping them by their general vibe cuts through the noise of memorizing individual card meanings.

Take a Physical Break Between Readings

When a heavy spread scrambles your brain, stop reading. Shuffle the deck, put the cards away, and walk away from the table. Trying to force clarity when your nervous system is on high alert leads to second-guessing and reading fear instead of intuition. Grab a glass of water, go outside for five minutes, and come back with fresh eyes.

Reading multiple heavy cards gets easier the more you experience them. As you practice observing these major themes without flinching, you will start to see them not as threats, but as helpful signposts during complicated times. Take your time sorting through the noise, and look at what your intuition is telling you about the bigger picture.
