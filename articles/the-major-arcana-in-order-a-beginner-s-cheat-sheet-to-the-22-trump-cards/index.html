When I first laid out a standard 78-card Tarot deck on my kitchen table, the Minor Arcana felt approachable enough. Cups, Wands, Swords, and Pentacles mapped neatly onto everyday human drama—love, creativity, conflict, and money. But then I hit the 22 cards of the Major Arcana.

Numbered from 0 to 21, these trump cards didn’t look like ordinary parlor cards. They featured stark, allegorical figures like a skeleton riding a pale horse, a lightning-struck tower, and a mysterious woman balancing the cosmos. I quickly realized that memorizing them individually was a dead end. To actually use them, I had to understand them as a sequence—a narrative arc often called “The Fool’s Journey.”

Here is my practical cheat sheet for learning the Major Arcana in order, without getting bogged down in dense historical lore.

The First Half: The Fool’s Awakening (Cards 0 to 9)

The first ten cards of the Major Arcana track the beginning of human consciousness. Think of Card 0, The Fool, as you at the start of a risky life choice—say, quitting your day job to freelance. You have raw potential, no baggage, and a one-way ticket into the unknown.

As The Fool stumbles forward, they meet figures who represent life’s early lessons and external forces:

  • The Magician (1) and The High Priestess (2): The balance of active willpower and inner intuition.
  • The Empress (3) and The Emperor (4): The archetypes of nurturing creation and structured authority.
  • The Hierophant (5) and The Lovers (6): Transitioning from institutional learning to personal moral choices.
  • The Chariot (7): Pulling disparate forces together to drive toward a single goal through sheer discipline.

Then comes a speed bump. Strength (8) teaches you that brute force fails against raw passion, requiring patience instead. Right after, The Hermit (9) forces you to turn off your phone, step away from the noise, and figure out what you actually believe when nobody is watching.

The Turning Point: Fate and Friction (Cards 10 to 14)

Once you emerge from the Hermit’s cave, you realize you aren’t running the whole show. The Wheel of Fortune (10) hits, representing sudden, uncontrollable shifts—like a company-wide layoff or an unexpected inheritance.

This middle block of the deck is where theory meets messy reality. If you want to change your life, you have to look at the collateral damage:

  • Justice (11): The cold, clear reality of cause and effect. Did you sign a contract without reading the fine print? Now you pay the piper.
  • The Hanged Man (12): The painful necessity of surrendering control and looking at a problem upside down.
  • Death (13): The literal end of a phase. If you are holding onto a miserable relationship because you are scared of being single, this card demands you let it die so something new can grow.
  • Temperance (14): The exhausting daily work of mixing opposing elements—like balancing a demanding career with personal health—without blowing a gasket.

The Breaking Point and Integration (Cards 15 to 21)

You cannot reach wisdom without breaking a few illusions. The final stretch of the Major Arcana deals with shadow work and societal structures.

The Devil (15) isn’t a red guy with a pitchfork; it’s the golden handcuffs of a secure job you despise or a toxic habit you use to cope with stress. When you finally snap those chains, you get The Tower (16)—the sudden, catastrophic collapse of a false foundation.

Once the dust clears, the heavy lifting of reconstruction begins:

  • The Star (17) and The Moon (18): Moving from raw trauma recovery into navigating deep, confusing anxieties.
  • The Sun (19): Simple, unadulterated clarity and joy after a long dark night of the soul.
  • Judgment (20): A serious personal audit where you answer for your choices and forgive your past mistakes.
  • The World (21): The final graduation. You have integrated all the lessons, survived the tower moments, and completed a major chapter of your life.

How to Practice This Sequence Today

If you want to move this knowledge from your head to your intuition, try a simple three-card daily pull using just the Major Arcana. Shuffle the 22 cards and draw three face-up on your desk.

Do not look them up individually. Instead, look at their numbers. Are you seeing early cards like The Fool and The Magician? You are likely in a phase of new beginnings and skill-building. Are you seeing high numbers like The Tower and The Judgment? You are in a period of intense restructuring and reckoning. By reading them left to right as a mini-story, you will watch the narrative logic of the deck click into place naturally.

As you start working with these cards, having a reliable framework makes all the difference in building real confidence with your deck.
